# Semantic review of measured v2.1 — cases 10–18

This review covers the nine final application records for cases 10–18 in the completed default `adapter-v2` acceptance run that began **2026-09-11 at 13:19:56 UTC**. The recorded schema version is `coached-five-sections-2.1`. I compared the original fictional source notes with the retained factual sections, suggestions, UNASSIGNED excerpts, and relevant raw outputs. The two longer cases took approximately 259.5 and 233.0 seconds respectively.

This is a separate semantic read-through of development acceptance cases, not an independent accuracy estimate. The examples were independently authored and excluded from model training, but their outputs were used during pipeline refinement. This report describes the frozen measured **v2.1** outputs; it does not evaluate subsequent v2.2 safeguards or targeted reruns.

## Main findings

The system often preserves uncertainty and proposes useful administrative questions. Case 11 keeps the rejected deletion rejected; case 12 retains the midnight rollover and distinguishes an update expectation from a completion deadline; case 15 preserves the disagreement between a reported resolution and a later observed failure. The longer drafts do not claim that maintenance was skipped, that an approved revision was established, or that a scan or verification was completed.

However, the measured pipeline still has material weaknesses:

- **Ideas can remain inside factual sections.** Case 16 places a possible impact in factual IMPACT and an invented proposed follow-up in factual PLAN. Cases 11 and 15 also retain author instructions or added recommendations as factual PLAN content. A source quotation can exist without supporting the generated statement or its placement.
- **Important source facts can be lost or left unassigned.** Cases 10 and 17 lose SITUATION because an otherwise valid source date was absent from the particular evidence quotes. Case 13 loses the main incident account because the model supplied an inaccurate quotation. Case 18 loses its last timestamp and its maintenance-form identifier from visible factual text.
- **Useful assistance is inconsistent.** Some questions clarify genuine gaps; others repeat supplied details, omit the needed plan, or reverse who is expected to provide an update.

## Longer-note coverage

### Case 17: binder discrepancy

The draft retains the broad sequence: observation at 07:41Z, contact at 08:18Z, callback at 11:26Z, and cabinet check/status message at 16:48Z. It keeps the case unresolved and does not infer incorrect attendance, approval, completed scanning, or a cancelled class.

The actual **Revision B cover versus Revision A first page** and course identifier survive only below the five sections in UNASSIGNED. The model's situation included the correct date from the source but did not cite the date in that item's evidence, triggering fallback. That fallback preserves substantial irrelevant physical-description material as well, making the key discrepancy harder to find. The photo reference **BINDER-FIC-204** and the completed act of retaining that limited photograph are absent from the visible draft and UNASSIGNED block.

Attribution needs attention. MB said the administrator **would need to compare** the records; the draft repeatedly says the administrator **would compare**, making an identified need sound more like a commitment. The callback source specifically says **MB** had not compared the master, while the draft assigns non-comparison to the **administrator**. The overall comparison-pending state is preserved, but those actors should not be silently substituted. A colleague's unaccepted scanning offer also appears within the Course Office contact paragraph, without evidence in the quoted call passages that this was part of those calls.

The closing message is retained, but the final text does not clearly reproduce the no-reply-before-leaving outcome or the lack of an agreed completion deadline. Its plan questions partially compensate by asking who will confirm the comparison and when the decision will be recorded.

### Case 18: newsletter receipt and unsigned checklist

The draft keeps recognizable references to both matters and retains the public receipt/filing events, the signature observation, both named offices, and the 17:06Z unresolved checklist update. It avoids making a causal relationship between the newsletter and maintenance paperwork. It does not turn the publisher into a confirmed contacted agency, assert a printer fault, or follow the embedded instruction to create a completed review/verification.

The main loss is the final **17:18Z newsletter-thread check with no new reply from VN**, plus the checklist identifier **PM-OFFICE-FIC-9**. Both remain inside source evidence attached to other generated statements, but neither appears in visible factual text or UNASSIGNED. Evidence availability alone is not an adequate readable chronology. SITUATION remains empty, and the CONTACTS section contains unrelated descriptions of saving a folder and observing a blank signature, while QD's actual contact is placed in ACTION and UNASSIGNED.

Assistance is incomplete. The main pass supplied possible administrative concerns, but these also remain mixed into factual IMPACT. Its ACTION recommendation was withheld because the cited basis combined noncontiguous text and attached 09:37Z to an untimed printer observation. The helper then generated a possible-impact suggestion with an ellipsis inside an allegedly exact quotation; source verification withheld it. Only a checklist-owner/response-time PLAN suggestion survives. The newsletter's missing owner and response timing do not receive their own useful follow-up.

## Interpretation and follow-up

UNASSIGNED excerpts preserve source visibility without silently assigning a doubtful sentence to a factual section. That is useful, but it does not make the five-section draft complete. Literal-retention failures should therefore be separated into details still visible below the draft, details present only in hidden evidence, and details omitted altogether.

The conditional helper demonstrably adds suggestions in cases 11 and 14. It does not resolve incorrect factual classification in case 16, missing incident content in case 13, or rejected evidence in case 18. Raw schema validity and exact quotation presence are insufficient measures of those behaviors.

The highest-value later regression checks are: keep author writing requests and generated ideas out of factual sections; preserve who must send versus receive an update; retain reported-versus-observed counts; keep essential long-note references and the last event visible; and avoid asking again for details already supplied. Subsequent pipeline changes require their own measured results rather than being credited retroactively to this run.
